An exemplary application: how computational tools can be applied to law and make invisible or barely visible aspects observable.

Master's thesis in Law · University of Sannio · academic year 2025/2026 · Antonella Ninfadoro · supervisor Prof. Nicola Lettieri

The site

I built this site with my supervisor, Nicola Lettieri, to make aspects of social reality, and of legal reality in particular, that are hard to bring into focus easier to see and understand.

The site visualises and explains what I defined in the theoretical part of the thesis, where I propose different ways of reading the law.

Computation makes it possible to measure and analyse aspects of social phenomena that, without these tools, might remain hidden or would take a very long time to study.

The choice of case comes from my own story. I went through university with a haematological cancer, and I know how hard it is to assert a right while undergoing treatment. My supervisor was one of the few who gave weight to my path, with great humanity: he opened the way for me, made it easier and allowed me to take the exam remotely. This site observes exactly that gap: between the protection the law promises and what a student actually manages to obtain.

In brief

Does the right to education actually reach students with disabilities?

Under Italian Law 104/1992, a person with a disability is someone with long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments which, in interaction with various barriers, may hinder full participation (art. 3, as reformed by Legislative Decree 62/2024). For all these students, art. 16 grants adapted examinations: extra time, aids, equivalent tests; at university, subject to an agreement with the lecturer.

The focus: students with disabilities and students with haematological cancer

A student being treated for haematological cancer is protected only through the disability route: they must be recognised as disabled or as having a civil disability after a medical assessment involving INPS, the national social security institute. The sources examined contain no study protection designed for them.

With the certificateLaw 104 or civil disability→the measures of art. 16 are a right
Without the certificate"disabling medical condition"→the university may take it into account, but is not obliged to
Without applyingdoes not request the assessment, for instance for fear it may weigh on an adoption→no certificate will come: the measures remain a concession

The third situation comes from a questionnaire: a student undergoing treatment did not request the assessment because she wishes to adopt and fears that a disability status may affect it. Whether disability status actually weighs on adoption must be checked against the adoption law (Law 184/1983) and practice.

The reform applies from 2025 in the pilot areas and throughout Italy from 1 January 2027 (Legislative Decree 62/2024, art. 40(2)). What counts is the province of residence or domicile. Rome has been a pilot area since 1 March 2026; Benevento has not. Until then, elsewhere, the 1992 wording applies: "physical, mental or sensory impairment, stable or progressive". List of provinces from INPS and the press: to be verified against the official act.

CNUDD guidelines 2024, pp. 11 (note 8), 21-22; University of Sannio guide, pp. 6-7. The legislation too ties protection to Law 104 or civil disability: fee exemption (Legislative Decree 68/2012, art. 9(2)), exceptions to in-person exams (Ministerial Decree 1835/2024, art. 5(2)). The only rule found that speaks of illness is the fee exemption for students who interrupt their studies because of "serious and prolonged illness" (art. 9(4)): during that period no academic step is allowed (art. 9(6)). Other universities' regulations and regional rules not yet examined.

The starting point

A law promises a protection. Can we observe whether it keeps that promise?

The framework observes the gap between what the law grants and what people manage to obtain.

Article 3(2) of the Italian Constitution requires the removal of obstacles that limit equality "in fact": the focus moves from the text of the rule to people's lives. The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (art. 24) speaks of "effective education". The framework is not a single method. Each technique comes into play after a precise question.

Level 1 · The provision

First we read the text, with tools that count and recognise

The text is measured: readability, structure, cross-references, terminology, formal regularity.

The first four questionnaires describe paths stalled for months, students giving up, remote lessons requested without reply. They are few cases, from people close to the author: they help build profiles and hypotheses, not estimate how often this happens. At the University of Sannio, uploading the documents "does not constitute formal activation of the procedure" (Unisannio guide, p. 7).

Level 4 · Possible scenarios

Simulation builds possible worlds: other rules, other timings, other information.

The first two levels mainly inform a judgement on formal quality; the third and fourth a judgement on effects. Data do not say that a law is just. They make observable the consequences that matter for that judgement, which remains entrusted to an explicit normative criterion.

What was done, what emerged

1 · the provision 39.0

Gulpease index of art. 16: difficult even for readers with a secondary-school diploma. The longest sentence, 75 words, is in paragraph 5-bis.

Operations: paragraphs, sentences, readability, cross-references, vocabulary, 7 legislative drafting rules; arts. 3, 4 and 13 for comparison.

It does not say whether the rule is fair or whether it works.

 2 · the relations 11 · 5 · 3

Written cross-references, amendments and steps of the path. Arts. 16 and 13 refer to each other; the path goes through the medical assessment.

Operations: cross-references extracted from the text and checked by a test, Normattiva update notes, path reconstructed from documentary sources.

More links do not mean a worse law.

 3 · the student's path 4 of 9

Exams for which the measures arrived in time, in the first questionnaires. In all four cases the path stalled for a long time.

Operations: steps documented with guidelines and sources, anonymous questionnaire, simulation under construction.

Four cases do not say how often this happens.

All prototypes are feasibility demonstrations: they show that the procedures work on the real text, not that they produce validated empirical knowledge. Computation adds tools to legal analysis without replacing it.
